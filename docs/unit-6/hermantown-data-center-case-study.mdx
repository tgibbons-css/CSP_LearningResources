## Unmasking the invisible cloud

The cloud can feel weightless when we tap an app. Its physical infrastructure occupies land and uses resources. Now examine Google’s proposed hyperscale data center in Hermantown, Minnesota. Your task is to reason from evidence, not to assume that the project is either inherently beneficial or inherently harmful.

:::info A dated case study, not a live project tracker
The pod fact cards use March–June 2026 sources, checked October 14, 2026.
:::

## How the jigsaw works

1. Form four expert pods: energy, water, economic/compute value, and community/land use.
2. Each pod uses its questions and linked sources to prepare **one supported claim, one uncertainty, and one proposed condition**.
3. Re-form into home groups with one representative from each pod. Share evidence before using the estimator.
4. Hold compute load constant while changing cooling architecture, then hold cooling constant while changing compute load. What changes? What does the model leave out?
5. Reach a consensus on **four measurable conditions** for the City and utility regulators.
6. Copy your group's work into the <a href="https://docs.google.com/document/d/1_sm_cGpVOs45VFb8ZrhnA9UxNItVV4-HrkgEJ1Prrjw/edit?usp=sharing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Shared Hermantown Case Study Google Doc</a>. :::tip Read across perspectives
For each claim, record the source, publication date, evidence, and what remains uncertain. Ask whether it describes a proposal, a commitment, a modeled scenario, or an observed outcome. Low direct cooling-water use does not settle questions about stormwater, habitat, indirect power-generation water, or community impacts.
:::

## Out-of-class reflection: The Cloud in Our Backyard

:::info Individual reflection
Compare your earlier mental model of “the cloud” with the physical infrastructure shown by <a href="https://datacenters.google/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Data Centers</a> and the Hermantown case study. Write an individual, evidence-based response addressing all three:

1. **Why this scale?** Use at least two of the **5 V’s**, or the **unstructured-data/distributed-computing** concepts from <Link to="/docs/unit-6/why-we-need-data-centers/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Why We Need Data Centers</Link>, to explain why facilities of this scale are being built. Explain the connections rather than simply naming terms.
2. **Which trade-offs?** Discuss specific electricity and water/cooling choices: the possible electricity penalty of low-water air or closed-loop cooling, Minnesota Power’s 700 MW wind/storage plan, and local watershed concerns. Cite the source and date of the claims you use. If you cite an estimator result, label it as an illustrative scenario and state its assumptions.
3. **Is the Value worth the footprint?** Give your own conclusion about whether the computing **Value** justifies the local physical footprint in St. Louis County. Explain who gains, who bears costs or risks, and what conditions or new evidence could change your position.

Include links to your evidence and acknowledge an uncertainty or counterargument. Use the class Brightspace instructions for the submission location and any due date or length requirement. Copying an activity summary does not submit this individual reflection.
:::
